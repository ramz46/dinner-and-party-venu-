The Modern Rehearsal Dinner Guide: Etiquette, Timelines & Menu Inspiration

By Genevieve Moreau • October 12, 2025 • 6 Min Read

Of all the events comprising a modern wedding weekend, the rehearsal dinner holds a uniquely tender position. It is the golden hour before the crescendo—the evening where two disparate family trees sit side by side, break artisanal bread, and share the stories that are simply too intimate for a 200-person ballroom.

Rule 1: The Guest List Dilemma

Historically, the rehearsal dinner was restricted strictly to the wedding party, their spouses, and the couple’s parents. In modern celebrations, however, couples are expanding this tradition into a warm welcome experience for out-of-town travelers.

If your budget or venue capacity is constrained, we recommend a two-part format: a private seated 3-course dinner for immediate bridal party members from 5:30 to 7:45 PM, followed by opening our Courtyard or Wine Lounge at 8:00 PM for all arriving guests to enjoy passed sparkling wine and sweet bites.

“The most memorable rehearsal dinners are not about performance; they are about presence. Give your guests space to exhale before the adrenaline of the wedding day.”

Rule 2: The Art of the Speech Order

One of the greatest benefits of the rehearsal dinner is speech freedom. Unlike the wedding reception, where speeches must be rigorously timed to keep the dance floor moving, the rehearsal dinner welcomes meandering, heartfelt storytelling.

  • The Welcome: The hosts (traditionally the groom's parents or the couple) open the evening as first courses are poured.
  • The Course Breaks: Space speeches between plates. Hear from childhood best friends after the pasta course, and college roommates before dessert.
  • The Open Mic: Keep an optional 15-minute open-floor window during digestifs for unexpected, impromptu family toasts.

Rule 3: Gifting and Run-of-Show Alignment

Use the final hour of your rehearsal dinner to present personalized thank-you gifts to your maid of honor, best man, bridesmaids, groomsmen, and parents. Doing this privately prevents wedding-day logistical clutter and creates an unforgettable, tearful memory that honors your closest confidants.
